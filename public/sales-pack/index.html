A note before the data

Read this first

SPANEX is an encapsulated soil microbial inoculant. It is not a fungicide, a fertiliser substitute, or a magic bullet. It is a delivery format for eight specific organisms with documented mechanisms of action and a peer-reviewed encapsulation patent.

This pack is structured the way an Australian agronomist would actually use it: organisms first, then how to apply them, then what we have evidence for, then what we are still building. The marketing-style summaries are at the back of the section dividers, not the front.

What you should expect from this pack

  • Eight one-page product sheets — strain identity, mechanism, per-strain CFU, agronomic threshold, crop fit.
  • An application guide that names water volumes, growth stages and re-entry intervals (nil, in our case).
  • A tank-mix compatibility statement that lists what we are not compatible with — because that's the bit you actually need.
  • A blank recommendation record sheet you can use in the paddock.
  • The five Indian field trials we have on file, statistically re-framed — not the farmer ratings.
  • Two open templates: our letter to the manufacturer asking for independent data, and our outreach to Australian universities.

What this pack does not contain

  • Australian university trial data — we don't have it yet. The §IV section explains how we are commissioning it.
  • FRAC / HRAC / IRAC codes — SPANEX is not a pesticide, so they don't apply.
  • PHI / MRL data — biofertilisers carry no withholding period; this is stated where required.
  • Yield-uplift superlatives. The numbers in §III are reproducible from the source FTR PDFs.
Honest framing

SPANEX has Indian field trial data and manufacturer CoAs. It does not yet have AU-specific independent trial data. We do not claim it does. We do recommend a paid pilot trial as the path to AU-specific data — terms on §20.

How to use this pack

The pack is print-bound. Tear out §14 (the recommendation record sheet) and use it in the paddock. Keep §15–18 as your evidence reference. Hand §20 (the letter / outreach) to your research lead if you want to co-fund a trial.

We brought the data, not the calendars.

Why 3 caps/ha (not 1)

Each strain in NPK Grow is at 3.3 × 10¹¹ CFU per cap — one third of the per-strain agronomic threshold. Apply 3 caps/ha to bring each individual strain to 10¹² CFU/ha threshold. Single-strain SPANEX SKUs require only 1 cap/ha.

Universal entry product. Default for cereals, pasture, cotton, hort. Add specialty SKUs (Aceto, Rhizo, Zinc Grow) where crop biology requires it.

Activation procedure (every cap, every time)

1 — Soak capsule(s) in 5 L lukewarm clean chlorine-free water · 6–8 hours (overnight). 2 — Stir / shake intermittently. 3 — Microbe count multiplies ~10× during activation. 4 — Dilute to 500 L total carrier water. 5 — Apply within 4 hours of dilution.

Conditions to avoid

Direct sunlight on mixed solution. Hot midday application. Chlorinated municipal water. Tank-mix with bactericides, copper-based fungicides, hydrogen peroxide. Apply before dry spell or onto cracked dry soil. Maintain 3–4 day gap from urea / DAP / MAP synthetic fertiliser application.

Jar-test protocol (5 min in the shed)

  1. Mix the proposed tank-mix at the same dilution as paddock rate, in 250 mL clean water.
  2. Add the activated SPANEX solution to the jar.
  3. Cap, invert 5 ×, leave 30 min.
  4. Check for: flocculation, clouding, layer separation, gas-off (foaming), heat.
  5. If clear or only mild clouding — proceed at small batch first.
  6. If precipitate, gas-off, heat, layer separation — DO NOT load tank.

Sheet 18 — Honest caveats

What the FTRs do not prove

Reproducibility limits

None of the 5 FTRs are statistically replicated. n = 1 farmer per trial. The two paired comparisons (Tomato, Grapes) are point estimates, not significance-tested differences. They are demonstrations, not proofs.

Programme isolation

All trials apply combined SRT programmes — SPANEX bio-capsules plus PROM, mycorrhiza, biocontrol kits, foliar sprays, humic / fulvic acid. SPANEX-isolated efficacy in isolation is not measured. The 8–9 % tomato yield uplift is attributable to the integrated programme, not to SPANEX alone.

Geographic transferability

All trials on Indian Black Soil (Vertisol-equivalent) in Chhattisgarh and Maharashtra. Australian regional / soil / climate / cultivar transferability is plausible but unverified.

What the disease-suppression in FTR #2 actually means

The Grapes trial documents zero bacterial / fungal infections in SPANEX-treated. However the IBP Kit (containing Trichoderma · Pseudomonas · Paecilomyces) is the dedicated biocontrol component of the programme — these strains, not the SPANEX bio-fertilisers, are likely doing most of the disease suppression work.

Path to AU validation

See §IV (page 22) — university outreach pack template + paid pilot trial offer. Recommended first AU pilot: a single-region single-crop paired-paddock trial mirroring the Indian Tomato design, in Vertosol cotton country (Liverpool Plains or Darling Downs) or in AACO Vertosol leucaena country.
